# Employer content repositioning

The existing site now addresses international employers as its primary audience, while retaining dedicated candidate guidance. This is an editorial balance, not a calculated percentage of every sentence.

## Content audit and classification

| Classification | Content | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: employer rewrite | Homepage supporting sections, About purpose and mission, general FAQ selection, services introduction, industry skill assessment and preparation, recruitment process, country introductions, contact introduction, shared contact heading, footer description and relevant SEO descriptions | Reframed around employer requirements, candidate evidence, Indian sourcing, technical assessment, documentation and mobilization. |
| B: candidate guidance | Candidate guide and its 20 FAQs, worker homepage preview, candidate contact tab, candidate enquiry actions, destination preparation sidebar and document links | Preserved. Labels still describe their candidate destinations. |
| C: technical or already employer-oriented | Workforce directory and all 18 detailed role guides, employer page, hiring tracks, industry workplace facts and role lists, country regulatory information, official references, resources, legal notices, enquiry forms and navigation | Preserved. General destination language and employment-terms guidance were adapted for employers without changing country-specific rules. |

The audit covered text-bearing components, data files, translation dictionaries, SEO descriptions and imported recruitment data. Existing neutral industry descriptions were retained rather than rewritten solely for stylistic consistency.

## Changes

- Updated 108 source passages representing 103 distinct English messages, with corresponding Hindi, Arabic, Hebrew, Russian, Portuguese, Czech, German, Japanese, Polish and Romanian translations.
- Reused six existing employer-relevant recruitment FAQs on the homepage. The dedicated candidate FAQ data is unchanged.
- All 14 industry guides now ask employers to assess relevant evidence, retaining technical distinctions such as operation versus maintenance, licensed nursing versus care work, vehicle licence recognition, equipment isolation, traceability and confidential project records.
- The eight process stages now describe the employer brief, sourcing, screening, eligibility, evaluation and selection, written terms, visa/travel coordination and mobilization. The existing diagram still has 14 nodes and the same connections.
- Incorporated varied Indian vocational backgrounds, different experience levels, individual qualification assessment and evidence of overseas project experience into existing paragraphs.
- Candidate selection remains an employer decision. Immigration approvals remain decisions of the relevant authorities.
- Rewritten non-metadata English passages grew from 10,252 to 10,552 characters, approximately 2.9%. Most individual blocks remain within 15% of their previous length. A small number of short headings and labels exceed that guideline for clarity; they were checked in responsive layouts.

## Preservation and validation

- JSX element order, classes, inline styles, links, image sources and event handlers match the pre-change snapshot.
- All 84 exported HTML documents retain the same ordered headings, sections, articles, paragraphs, list items, form elements, buttons and images, with identical links and image sources.
- Stylesheets, route inventory, deployment configuration, dependencies, brand configuration, form mechanisms, candidate guide and full workforce role prose are unchanged.
- Lint, TypeScript checking and production build passed. Build generated the existing 86 Next.js pages.
- Translation validation passed: 1,717 catalog entries present in every supported non-English locale, with valid placeholders.
- Browser verification passed: 354 assertions and 176 viewport checks across eight key routes in all 11 languages, including Arabic/Hebrew direction, actual translated passages, tabs, keyboard FAQ operation and diagram structure. Additional homepage checks covered 360, 768 and 1,024 px widths. Mobile and desktop screenshots were inspected.
- Existing workforce static checks passed: 18 role guides, 20 workforce/enquiry routes, 1,313 local links and 84 images; role pages remain in the sitemap.
- All 214 existing workforce browser regression checks passed, including role navigation, filters, employer enquiry validation, download behaviour, language switching and responsive layouts.

Detailed workforce prose remains English with the pre-existing translated disclosure. The new marketing copy is translated in all supported languages; this change does not claim to have translated the full role guides.

## Business verification still required

1. The previous claim that international recruitment builds on work across Indian states was replaced with the confirmed sourcing scope. Historical experience still requires supporting business records before any future claim.
2. The absolute assertion that every candidate is background-checked, skill-tested and verified was replaced with agreed role-specific checks and review before selection.
3. Existing team names, role assignments, specialties and displayed portraits require owner verification. Years-of-experience fields remain in source data but are not displayed by the current team component.
4. Work certificates remain clearly marked draft templates with issuer and project details pending. They are not evidence of completed work or accreditation.
5. Existing training, post-deployment support and replacement arrangements need confirmation in the actual service scope or written agreement. No new service guarantees were added.
6. Verified contact details, office information and the employer enquiry email are still unconfigured. Existing honest fallback and download behaviour remain in place.
7. Legacy testimonial data contains placeholder clients and unsupported placement claims. It is not imported by the rendered site; do not publish it without substantiation. Legacy source data should not be treated as verified company history.
8. Existing country-specific legal statements and official links were retained, not newly legally verified. The destination guides do not establish company licensing or active operations in those countries.

No deployment or domain registration was performed as part of this content update.
